BRAND GUIDE

Every legend needs someone to pour the drinks.

Heroes Not Included is the village behind the adventure: the innkeeper, the smith, the healer, the tinker and the folks who know where everything is buried. The heroes get the glory. You get the tab, the gossip and, if you play it right, the whole story.

The story in one breath

Every adventure starts in a tavern. Ours starts behind the bar. You are the person the party walks past on the way to glory: the one who hears the rumours, posts the quests, patches the armour and keeps the lights on. The adventurers think they are the main characters. They are not wrong, exactly. They just aren't the ones running the place.

Who we are

A warm, cheeky village of people with jobs, secrets and a long memory for who didn't tip.

Who we are not

Not a hero's journey. No chosen ones, no destiny, no swords on the logo. The adventure is the weather; the village is the story.

How it feels

A busy common room at dusk: warm, a little loud, someone laughing, and one person in the corner who is definitely up to something.

How we sound

We sound like the innkeeper: warm, quick, a bit worn at the edges, and always listening. Fun first, fantasy as seasoning. If a line needs a glossary or a funny accent to land, it is too much.

1

Warm first

Welcome people in before explaining anything. We are the lit window on a cold night.

2

Dry, never mean

Heroes are the punchline. Players never are. Tease the entitled adventurer, not the person who just burned the stew.

3

Plain words, fantasy furniture

Quests, coin, ale and rumours are the seasoning. Sentences stay short and modern. No thee, thou, hath or "ye olde".

4

Sly about secrets

We always seem to know a bit more than we say. Hint, wink, raise an eyebrow. Never state what a character couldn't know.

Clarity beats cute on anything you press. A button says what it does. The joke goes in the line underneath.

The emblem

A tavern sign, hung by the people who run the place. Where a hero's crest would carry crossed swords, ours carries the tools of the trades.

  1. The signboard. The village's own sign. This is the NPC world, and you're the one who hung it up.
  2. The "!" The quest marker every adventurer knows. Above your head, it means you have work for them.
  3. The tankard. The innkeeper's seat: where rumours land and jobs get handed out.
  4. Hammer and chef's knife. The smith and the cook, crossed where a crest would carry swords.
  5. The flame. The hearth. It's warm in here, so stay a while.
  6. 24 runes. One for every hour of a game day, engraved into the iron.

Using it

Ember is rare

One ember thing per screen: the quest marker, the call to action, a lit window. If everything glows, nothing is hiring.

Dark ground, bone text

The brand lives in the dark of the common room. Oak Deep or Cellar for the page, Bone for text, Parchment for the quieter lines.

Clear space and size

Keep the height of the quest marker (about one fifth of the emblem) clear on every side. Full-colour emblem down to 32 px; below that, use a simplified icon without the runes.

Never

Put a hero on it. No swords, shields, helmets or dragons: the brand is the world around them. Don't recolour the ribbon, stretch the lettering, or set the emblem on a busy photo without the ink version.
