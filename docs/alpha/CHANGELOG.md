# Playtest changelog

What changed in each playtest build, newest first. The release workflow posts the newest
section to Discord's #builds with the build, and the packages ship this file as CHANGELOG.txt.
Write it for testers: what they'll notice, not how it's done.

Playing together always needs everyone on the same build. Saves and characters carry over
between builds unless a section's **Compatibility** line says otherwise.

## 0.1.0-playtest.29

More for the musician.

- **The musician's hat** is worn now: a felt hat with a feather. It comes off and goes down upturned at
  your feet while you play, and back on your head after.
- **The playing panel shows the instrument in your hands:** your fingers on the pipe's holes, the lute's
  and fiddle's strings fretted and ringing (and the fiddle's bow), the drum struck where you strike it.
  A first pass: tell us how it looks.
- **The musician hears things:** adventurers listening to your music sometimes talk about a quest. Your
  playing panel lists the talk you've heard, and F at the innkeeper passes it on.

## 0.1.0-playtest.28

Two changes for the co-op round.

- **Tip the musician:** F at a playing musician's hat tosses a coin in, as often as you like. The
  musician sees who gave it.
- **Wounds stop bleeding by themselves** after a few hours, even untended. A town that all went down in
  a raid now wakes up hurt instead of dead by morning. The deepest wounds still kill, and a wound still
  wants the empath or a bandage to heal properly.

## 0.1.0-playtest.27

The musician joins the town, Millbrook grows a hill to the east, work for two gets proper scenes, and
raiders now go for your purse.

**The musician** (only if someone plays it)
- A new trade: wander in from the road with a tin pipe and a dagger, and play the pipe, the lute, the
  fiddle and the hand drum. Play free on the note keys, or open your tune book: play a tune's opening
  and your hands carry the rest while you keep the beat (F) and swell the long notes (Shift).
- Your hat goes down at your feet: listeners tip, call for tunes, and dance. Music at the inn draws
  custom, and a party sent off with a tune leaves heartened. Strings drift out of tune; tune them by ear.
- The inn gives you a room on the house. Easygoing: a good trade for a first round. Four tutorial chapters.

**Work for two**
- Every helper's panel is now a picture of the work: the striker sees the anvil and the smith's
  hammer winding up ("Here it comes!" then "Now!"); the keeper sees the body on the pallet lifting
  toward the grave's glow and their own hands holding it down; the pitman looks down on the log and
  the saw between the two of you; the lifter sees the wagon tipping on its far wheel.
- Whoever asked for help sees their helper at it, under their own panel.
- F anywhere near work that wants a hand now lends it (the busy woodshop kept offering everything else).
- The keeper's pull is slower, the lift steadier, and hanging on a moment after a pit-saw stroke no
  longer binds the saw.

**The town**
- Millbrook grows east to a hill with a mine (for a miner trade that's coming; it's switched off for now).
- The smithy moved off the west road. It now keeps bronze, copper and tin, and the carter brings more,
  so bronze pieces can be made. A piece is "hot enough" by the colour you see.
- Planks for what's made, charcoal baked at the smithy's kiln, cloth for the empath's bandages.
- **Raiders rob the fallen:** a raider who puts you down cuts half your silver and runs for the edge.
  Catch them before they're away and they drop it.
- Raids are sized to the players: a solo town meets smaller bands, and each player makes them bigger.
- The festival is shorter, the crier's tale quicker, and you can cheer every honour as well as every toast.
- A late joiner can take a trade nobody's playing, the musician included.

**Fixed**
- No "Raid!" banner when a trip-bell rang out of sight of most of the town.
- The labourers no longer carry off the musician's hat.
- A shrine pallet clicked by the musician opened the music instead of the bed.
- The triggers no longer swing a weapon while you play an instrument on a gamepad.
- A save that came back different on another player's machine.

## 0.1.0-playtest.26

The carpenter goes into the woods, the town looks after its fallen, adventurers shop for themselves,
and a long list of fixes from the Steam Deck playtest.

**The carpenter**
- Fell trees by hand: aim the fall, cut the notch, then the back cut. A tree comes down where it
  leans and lands on whoever's under it. Buck the trunk into logs (great logs slow whoever carries
  them), saw a log into planks, and split logs into firewood at the woodshop's new chopping block.
- The town burns firewood now, and the carter brings a little less than it needs: the carpenter
  makes up the rest.
- Worn bows, cudgels and staves go to the woodshop to be mended (they used to go unmended), and
  crossbows to the tinker. An archer with no quiver asks the woodshop for one aloud.
- Wagons are shared: the wheel is the tinker's, the woodwork the carpenter's (a new axle, a sprung
  board nailed with the claw hammer). Furniture lost in a fire is joined again at the bench and set
  back where it stood.
- The carpenter starts with a woodcutter's axe. New tutorial chapters: "Fell a tree" and "Split firewood".

**Work for two**
- The keeper: someone holds the body while the empath calls them back. The pitman: the second sawyer
  under a great log. The lifter: holds a wagon level while its new axle goes on. Bots join in too.

**The town**
- No more hunger or tiredness meters. Sleep two hours and you're well rested; eat and you're well
  fed (the better the dish, the more). Both make you a little quicker. Going without costs nothing.
- When someone collapses nearby, the two closest drop everything to bind the bleeding and carry
  them to a pallet or a bed.
- Adventurers buy off a wares shelf themselves, whether or not you're there. Finding nothing for
  them, they may ask you to make a piece by name.
- The season reacts to how the town's doing: crises come sooner or later and harder or softer, and
  small incidents (a fever, rats, a traveller's errand) fill the quiet stretches.
- The healer is now the empath. The tinker can set a trip-bell on the road: without one, you only
  know a raid's coming when someone sees it.
- Trade with another player face to face: F at them with something in hand gives it, with empty
  hands asks to trade.

**From the Deck playtest**
- Start closes the game menu as well as opening it.
- The controller's buttons are named as you know them: Select and Start, not View and Menu.
- The tutorial names the controller's buttons on a controller: the wheel (RB) for your shop, quests
  and the gift, LT where it said right-click, RT and the right stick where it said click.
- The lock pick's hints say which way on the stick: up and down for tension, left and right for the pin.
- Playing alone, time stops while a tutorial card is open. Press Got it (or B) to carry on. You can
  turn this off in Settings.
- Practice customers wait however long you take. "Set it up again" sends the earlier ones away,
  and what they handed you goes with them.
- Mini-game windows open in front of everything, in the middle of the screen, and shrink to fit a
  small screen.
- Hover over a minimap pin or edge arrow to see what it points at. Your home's arrow has a little house on it.
- Character creation: a Random button for the name alone; the folk tooltip describes the folk you're
  pointing at; "Start from" is now "Starting look" (it's only your look, your trade comes later).
- More, then Characters, then Back returns to More.
- Each trade is marked easygoing, steady or busy where you choose it.
- The coin flip, hand and all, shows in front of a beard, and no longer appears on the back of your head.

**Also**
- Only a shop's own trade can take its orders: an empath can't take the inn's drink orders any more.
- An adventurer staying the night keeps back the price of a bed instead of spending it on gear.
- Lifting a wagon: A and D leaned it the wrong way.

## 0.1.0-playtest.25

A sixth trade, the carpenter; wares shelves in every shop; the innkeeper's rations come from the cook;
and the town's hands remembered behind every hero.

**The carpenter**
- Millbrook's sixth trade, at the woodshop: logs hewn square with the axe and pit-sawn into planks,
  bows scraped to an even bend and strung, and quivers of arrows sighted, shaved, headed and
  fletched for the parties' archers. A badly made arrow doesn't show until it's shot.
- The carpenter helps the tinker brace the bridge when it's down, and puts up the festival.
- Tutorial chapters for the saw, the bow and the quiver; practice customers are archers.

**Selling your wares**
- The smithy, the shrine, the tinker's and the woodshop each have a wares shelf. Put a blade, a
  charm, a contraption or a quiver on it (F at the shelf with it in hand, or right-click it in your
  pack: "Put on the wares shelf") and it's for sale at once at a fair price.
- Open the shelf to change a price or take a piece off sale. Only you can take your wares down.
- Things set down on the floor aren't for sale any more: use the shelf.

**The inn and the kitchen**
- The cook no longer sells to adventurers. The innkeeper sends for a hamper from the bar (the
  button on the bar card), the cook packs it and puts it up on the hatch, and the innkeeper unpacks
  it behind the bar. The rations go up for sale on the merchandise shelf there.
- A hamper costs what's in it: the cook's price is for a full one.
- In Reedmere a labourer runs the hamper over from the cookhouse for a couple of silver. Parties
  setting out buy their rations now; they don't take them free.

**Behind every hero**
- Each quest remembers whose work went with the party: the blade, the armour, the charm, the
  rations, the quiver. The reveal tells it, hidden flaws and all.
- At the festival a party back in triumph names someone in its toast.
- The greatsword takes two: the smith taps where the striker's sledge comes down. Join in with F
  at the anvil. Call "Help!" from the quick-say wheel at work for two, and a villager who likes you
  may come.

**Also**
- Character traits are gone: no Traits tab, nothing weighing on mood.
- Esc closes the newest window first, before the game menu.
- Customers in the practice yard bring enough silver for what they came for.

## 0.1.0-playtest.24

Workstation windows that fit on the screen, and a right click and scrolling for the controller.

**Talking without typing**
- Hold U to open a wheel at the mouse. The inner ring shows a feeling: wave, bow, cheer, laugh,
  point, shrug, weep or angry. The outer ring calls out: help, over here, thanks, aye, nay, coming,
  fire or raiders. Move toward one and let go.
- An emote shows in a bubble over your head, to anyone who can see you. Pointing marks the spot
  under the mouse.
- Calls are said aloud like anything you type, so they carry only as far as a voice does. The
  alarms are shouted.
- With a controller, hold RB for the action wheel and press LB to turn to Show and Say.
- Bot players wave when they greet someone.

**Workstations**
- The forge is split into tabs: Make, Smelt and Mend. Each tab shows only when you have something
  for it. When a customer's piece is waiting, the forge opens on Mend and its tab is marked.
- The forge's Begin button and the other workstations' Make button now sit at the bottom of the
  window, always in view. Only the list above them scrolls.
- A list with more below it fades out at the bottom and shows a small arrow.
- These windows stay on the screen as they grow, instead of running off the bottom.

**With a controller**
- Tap LT to right-click where the pointer is: an item's menu, for one. Hold LT and push the right
  stick to scroll the window under the pointer instead.
- The controls card (L3) lists the pointer's controls when you're on a controller.

## 0.1.0-playtest.23

Point and click with a controller, quieter cards at the bar and the stew pot, and a tutorial card that folds away.

**Pointing with a controller**
- When your weapon isn't drawn, the right stick moves a pointer and the right trigger clicks. It
  works on anything a mouse click does: people, things in the world, and any button in a window or
  on the title screen. A still uses or works whatever is in front of you.
- The pointer fades a few seconds after you stop using it. Moving the mouse or touching the trackpad
  takes over from it.
- It's off while your weapon is drawn, while you hold a full bucket, at the archery butts, while the
  action wheel is open and in camera mode, since the stick and trigger have other jobs there.
- With the stick now pointing, it no longer turns your character when your weapon is sheathed. You
  face the way you walk.

**At the bar and the stew pot** (with Simple HUD on)
- The bar's card is down to one row: a button for each named drink, and a House mix button that shows
  its ingredients only when you open it. Hover a drink to see what goes in it, or why you can't make
  it yet.
- The stew pot's card shows how it's cooking and its buttons. Hover to see what's in it and how it tastes.

**The tutorial**
- Press "Got it" to fold the tutorial card away while you work. It becomes a small Tutorial button at
  the top of the screen that shows which step you're on (hover it to read the step). Press it to open
  the card again. The card opens by itself when the next step comes up.

## 0.1.0-playtest.22

A shorter title menu, a quieter screen, and a plain way to take an order. All from playing on a Steam Deck.

**The title menu**
- The main menu is down to what you'll want most: Continue, New round, Tutorial, Play with friends,
  Settings, More and Quit. Hosting and joining are under Play with friends. Characters, saves,
  replays, bug reports and the credits are under More. It fits a Steam Deck's screen without scrolling.
- Continue only shows when there's a save to go back to.
- With a controller, the menu's first entry is picked when you arrive. Pressing down no longer skips it.

**A quieter screen**
- A new setting, Simple HUD (Settings, Interface), is on by default. It shows only what needs you:
  - Hunger, rest and mood appear on your card when they're getting low. Hover your portrait to see them all.
  - Your warmth shows when you're cold or hot, and the weather when it's cold out.
  - The town's standing is behind the season line (hover it). Dread shows on its own once it starts
    to weigh on the valley.
  - No compass, since the minimap already marks north.
  - The Shop panel starts closed, and its tab is marked when a customer is waiting.
- Turn Simple HUD off to see everything all the time, as before.

**Taking orders**
- Walk up to a customer waiting at your shop and press F (A on a controller) to take their order.
  The prompt over your head says whose order it is and what it pays. This works for the smith's
  mending and the tinker's strongboxes and curios.
- A customer nobody has seen to yet gets a "waiting" tag, with an arrow at the screen's edge when
  they're out of view.
- In the Shop panel, the button that takes an order now says "I'll do it" instead of being a plain check mark.

## 0.1.0-playtest.21

Pausing, a map of the town, a camera you can turn freely, and tables you can walk across.

**Pausing**
- **Alone:** the game menu (Esc, Start) now stops time while it's open, and the pause key (P, or
  Pause) holds it until you press it again.
- **Together:** the pause key asks everyone. Time stops once every player agrees, and anyone can say
  "carry on" to turn it down. A call nobody answers lapses after 30 seconds. To start again, everyone
  says they're ready. Someone who drops out can't keep the town paused.

**Finding your way**
- A minimap in the bottom right shows the town around you, turned the way your camera faces, with
  a pin on everything the edge arrows point at. Press M for the whole town map. It shows streets
  and buildings, not people.

**The camera**
- Hold the middle mouse button and drag to turn the view to any angle.
- The turn keys (Z/X, the d-pad) now turn it an eighth of the way round instead of a quarter.

**Tables**
- Up on a table, you can walk its whole length and across a big square one without getting stuck in
  the middle, and stepping off no longer snags you on its edge.
- Clicking from a table no longer picks things on the floor upstairs (the bed above the inn).

## 0.1.0-playtest.20

A tutorial for every trade, the fiddly mini-games made fairer, and a camera and replays for
watching a season back.

**The tutorial**
- "Practice yard" on the title is now **Tutorial**, marked "start here" until you've played. Pick a
  trade and it walks you through it: what the trade is about, the basics (walking, your pack, your
  shop, the controls card), then the trade's main work, one step at a time. Each step points at
  what to use (with an arrow at the screen's edge when it's off screen) and lights up the button
  to press. Stuck for a while, it offers a fuller hint and sets the scene up again.
- After the main chapters you can start a round in that trade, try the extra chapters, or practise
  freely.
- The "Getting started" tips box in a round is gone: everything it said is now in the tutorial.
- The menu's descriptions always show, so the list no longer shifts as you move over it.

**Mini-games**
- **Pouring:** tip the tankard and the ale runs down its side with less foam, but tipped, it runs
  over the low lip sooner. Tip it early, bring it upright near the top. The picture now holds the
  tankard under the tap the way you'd expect.
- **Feeling for traps:** stop where the probe catches and hold still: the surer your read, the
  wider the green. Hold F to press in, then Space while the needle's in the green to flick the
  spring out. Outside the green, it goes off. The mouse no longer drags the probe while you press.
- **Laying on hands:** while drawing a wound, point the mouse where the needle should be and it
  follows, settling on the spot. With the keys it's a little less easy than it was.
- **The hammer and the lock:** both are more forgiving of a press that's a moment late, and the
  lock's pins no longer drop while the needle shows green.
- Clicking outside a mini-game no longer walks you away from it.

**The inn and the kitchen**
- The cook ladles straight into a clean bowl from the kitchen shelf; the inn has its own wash tub
  behind the bar.
- A red X on an order turns it away: "Sorry, we're out." They take it better than being left
  waiting.

**The season**
- The dragon now comes on the last morning, and is gone before the festival is set up.
- Raids are a little stronger. Rest in bed brings blood back much faster.
- Millbrook's gardens have real gates, and none sit on the road.

**New**
- **Replays:** every round is kept, to watch again from the reveal or from Replays on the title:
  scrub the timeline, jump to each crisis, follow anyone.
- **Camera mode** (the ` key, or R3): fly the camera free for filming. In a round it still only
  shows what your character could see.

## 0.1.0-playtest.18

Fixes from our first outside playtester: a menu on Esc, easier ways into buildings, and a tidier
kitchen.

**Menus**
- Esc now opens the game menu (save, load, settings, controls, back to the title) when there's
  nothing else for it to close. Press it again to close the menu. It also backs out of settings,
  saves, credits and the recipe book.
- The trade descriptions on "Which trade will you take?" no longer run off the edge. Longer ones
  wrap, and the highlight grows to hold them.

**Getting in and out**
- Clicking a building's roof walks you inside, through its door. Before, only a click on the
  doorway itself did.
- Standing at a building's door keeps it open to view, so you can see the doorway and the room
  as you come and go. The healer's shrine, whose door faces away from the camera, was the worst.

**The kitchen**
- The pot, spit, oven, prep table, butcher block and bar now share one card at the bottom of the
  screen, instead of each opening a panel that stacked up over the kitchen. It shows the station
  you're facing, with a tab for each other one in reach.

## 0.1.0-playtest.17

Reedmere rebuilt as a lake town, rare companies on the road, and Millbrook's inn with windows and a
double door. The world also shimmers much less as the camera moves.

**Reedmere**
- Redrawn as a town on the long lake. Houses crowd on piles round an open harbour, with a
  water-gate canal and one long causeway out to a headland.
- The moot hall stands on the far quay and the forge on a stone pier. The inn and the cookhouse
  face each other across the water-gate.
- The festival is held on boats lashed together in the harbour, round a mast.
- Rowboats and reeds sit on the water, lanterns burn on the quays all night, and every open deck
  edge has a rail.

**Rare companies**
- Now and then a company comes in place of a party: the Greyhorn Company or the Company of the
  Long Road. They're their own folk, sized to the number of players, and they take only their own
  errand.

**Millbrook's inn**
- A lower roof, so the timbered upper storey shows to the street. The gables are calmer and the
  chimneys taller.
- A double door at the front, with a step before each.
- Windows on both floors. By day they let light into the room behind them, brightest beside the
  window. After dark, a lit room's windows glow from the street.

**Looks**
- Walls, thatch and timbering no longer flicker as the title screen turns or when you zoom out.
  Roof edges and beams are smoother too.
- New setting: Settings > Display > Anti-aliasing (Off, 2x, 4x, 8x). It's 4x by default and 2x
  on a Steam Deck. On an older PC, try 2x or Off. The change applies the next time you start the
  game.

**Fixes**
- Doors on the ground floor of two-storey buildings (the inn's front door among them) weren't
  drawn at all, so you saw the floor through an empty doorway. They are now.

## 0.1.0-playtest.16

A second town, inn rooms with keys, the mouse in every mini-game, and townsfolk with tempers.

**Reedmere, a town on stilts**
- Pick it when you start a new round (the town choice was greyed out; it works now). Houses on piles
  over a lake, joined by boardwalks and one causeway to the shore. If a raid brings the causeway
  down, the town is cut in two until the tinker mends it. Buckets are filled at dipping posts.
- It's harder than Millbrook for now. The cook has a cookhouse on the shore and the innkeeper an
  inn without a kitchen out at the landing. The innkeeper orders hampers of food at the
  cookhouse hatch (F) and unpacks them on the warming shelf behind the bar. That shelf feeds
  lodgers at breakfast and supper, and gives parties rations for the road.

**The inn**
- Rooms have locked doors and keys. The keys hang on a board behind the bar: hand one over to let
  the room, and sign the guest into the book. Spare keys are in the storeroom. Once a guest leaves,
  turn the room round: strip and make the bed, empty the slops, fill the jug from a bucket.
- Drinks can be served across the bar, and anywhere behind the bar reaches its shelves.
- Every order you've taken gets a marker, and order cards have a check to take them.
- Travellers stop for a night or two. Guests go to bed at their own hour.
- You can drink too, and it goes to your head.
- A rowdy drunk may start a fist fight.
- The inn has new art: stone below, oak above jutting over the street, deep thatch, new furniture.

**The mini-games**
- The mouse works in every game that takes it:
  - At the forge you wind the hammer back to the mark and bring it down.
  - The cook stirs by circling, plucks by dragging and aims the cleaver.
  - Chopping is now by the fingers: draw them back a slice, then bring the knife down.
  - You point the brush to clean.
- The cook's games show one clear picture each, and the mixing cues are easier to read.
- Buckets are swung and thrown like a bow, and you can throw on the move.

**Townsfolk and adventurers**
- Adventurers have tempers. Drink, ghosts and bad food change what they do.
- Bots play every trade's whole job: the kitchen, the bar and rooms, errands from the board. They
  also slip up, put their work on show, and pitch in with buckets and the downed.
- AI trades pin errands on the notice board for the labourer.

**Ghosts**
- More to do once you're dead: startle or beckon beasts, possess one, leave frost words, send
  dreams, and unnerve raiders. Calling a ghost back is easier to find.

**Fixes**
- The quest board runs from easy to hard. Fixes for healing, truing, wood, quests, flicker and the
  downed. Deaths in town are counted, and feasts give the cook more time.
- Rounds run faster.

## 0.1.0-playtest.15

The dragon reworked, the healer easier to read, and fixes from the first solo playtest.

**The dragon**
- In a round it now harries the town from the air first: three passes over roofs and trees,
  breathing fire as it goes over. Then it lands by the well and stays down, going after anyone out
  in the open and setting buildings alight when nobody comes out. Hurt it, or gather a crowd in the
  open, and it comes down early.
- It won't follow you indoors (it doesn't fit through doors): it burns the building instead.
- You can see it flying overhead from anywhere outdoors.
- A "Dragon!" banner announces it, for the whole town.
- Water works on it: a bucket on or beside it puts its fire out for a while (it can only bite), and
  four soakings drive it off. Only once it's landed: it flies out of a bucket's reach.
- Its breath looks and sounds the part: a jet of flame that cools as it goes, smoke, scorches, and
  the screen rumbles when it's close (Settings, Screen shake).
- In the practice ring it stays inside the fence, keeps after you instead of waiting, and mixes
  bites, claw rakes and breaths. Tell us whether it's still a fair fight.

**Water**
- A thrown bucket flies in an arc, splashes, and steams where it hits fire.

**The healer**
- Laying on hands: your palms glow and the hum rises as you near a wound, and a warmth blooms when
  you find one.
- The gift's window no longer gets squeezed by the inspector, so the wounds you carry stay in view.
- Weave (the button at the top of the gift) now mends the worst wound you carry that it fits,
  instead of finding nothing.

**The practice yard**
- Six healer lessons are now four, and every trade's lessons are plain step-by-step instructions.
- The panel is shorter: the intro text is gone.

**The tinker**
- Bracing the bridge: you move the level yourself (A/D) and lift or lower the stone under it.

**Co-op and crises**
- You no longer see the other player's messages ("You draw the wound…") as your own pop-ups.
- The "Fire!" banner reaches the whole town; "Rats in the larder" goes to the cook.

## 0.1.0-playtest.14

Smarter footwork in a fight, for townsfolk, raiders and the dragon alike.

**Fights**
- A hurt fighter drops back and lets a friend take a turn, unless they're the last one standing or
  the foe is on them. One who's broken backs away facing the foe before turning to run.
- The dragon no longer just sits there between breaths: on the ground it circles whoever comes at
  it and lunges in when it's ready to bite. It's harder to drive off; tell us how it feels.
- Up to four can press the dragon at once.
- Unarmed townsfolk out in the street gang up on a raider with their fists when there are enough
  of them. Alone or in twos they still run; indoors they stay put.

## 0.1.0-playtest.13

A tuning build: the healer reworked, fights that hold up, and a way to tell us how the season feels.

**Telling us how it went**
- Press **F11** whenever something feels off, too hard, too easy, broken, or good. It takes a
  screenshot and asks for a line about it. Rebind it in Settings, Controls (on a Steam Deck, a back
  button in Steam Input works well).
- Each round now keeps a log of how it went: Renown and Dread, the crises, and the connection. At
  the season's end, press "Open playtest folder", zip it up and send it to us with your notes. Turn
  it off in Settings, "Playtest logs".

**The healer**
- Laying on hands is one sitting: skim the patient for warmth, press still to read the wound
  through your hand, keep pressing to draw it out. Move while pressing and they flinch.

  basin that keeps a bound wound from festering. New practice-yard lessons for each.
